A Call to Protect
What Gives Us Life

At Eco Village, we believe every human being has the power — and the responsibility — to care for our planet.

Nestled in the lush hills of Hilo on the Big Island of Hawaiʻi, our ranch is more than a home — it is a wildlife habitat, a reforestation project, and a place where visitors can stay and learn. Every building here is designed to be fully self-sustained — so we take from the earth only what we need, and give back without polluting Mother Nature.

We exist to inspire others to do something better: to help one another, to protect the environment, and to create genuine happiness — for ourselves, for our communities, and for the world we share.

Mau and Paola have planted 2,500 trees across Hawaiʻi — restoring the land tree by tree and welcoming wild birds back to a place they can call home again.

Panchito is a wild Nēnē who chose Eco Village as his home. Mau planted the fruits he loves and built a pond just for him — and Panchito keeps coming back. He greets guests on the lawn, swims in his pond, and reminds us every day why we restore this land.

A Place Where Life Thrives

Cows, sheep, and free-range chickens share the land with Panchito, a wild Nēnē who has made our ranch a second home. Every creature here is part of a greater ecosystem — one that mirrors the balance we seek in our own lives. Visitors leave with a deeper understanding of what it means to live alongside nature, not against it.

Restoring the Land

2,500 Trees — and Counting

Mau and Paola have planted 2,500+ trees across Hawaiʻi. Wild birds are already returning to nest among the branches — and when guests visit, they are welcome to help plant more and experience the joy of reforesting Mother Nature. Every tree is a promise to future generations.

This ranch exists to heal the land, not just live on it.

Day after day, Mau and Paola plant, water, and care for native and fruit trees across their property and beyond. As the forest grows back, so does the wildlife — birds, pollinators, and creatures like Panchito find food, shelter, and peace here again.

Every Airbnb stay directly supports this mission. Guest income goes toward buying more trees, maintaining the ranch for them to thrive, and continuing the work of bringing birds back to the land.

  • 2,500+ trees planted in Hawaiʻi
  • 100% of vacation rental income reinvested in the land
  • Wild birds returning as the habitat grows
Self-Sustained Architecture

Built to Give Back,
Not Take Away

Every structure at Eco Village is designed to sustain itself — generating its own water and power, producing no waste that harms the land. We built this way so we never pollute Mother Nature.

Architecture in Harmony with the Land

From the ground up, our buildings were planned to operate independently of outside utilities. No fossil fuels, no chemical runoff, no burden on the environment — only a closed loop of rain, sun, and wind working together.

Self-sustained architecture is not a luxury here. It is a promise: that human shelter can exist without harming the world that shelters us.

  • 100% off-grid — no connection to polluting power lines
  • Self-contained water cycle — collect, purify, reuse
  • Zero harm to soil, air, and surrounding wildlife
  • Built to last generations, not seasons
💧

Rainwater Harvesting

We collect rainwater from the sky and purify it for daily use — drinking, cooking, bathing, and nurturing the land. Every drop is a gift we never take for granted.

☀️

Solar Power

Hawaiʻi's abundant sunshine powers our entire Eco Village through solar panels. Clean, quiet electricity — straight from the sun above.

The Founder

Meet Mau

A dreamer, a builder, and a guardian of the earth — together with his wife Paola Rochabrun, who shares the daily life and heart of Eco Village.

Mau did not simply arrive in Hawaiʻi — he sailed here. Over three years, he crossed oceans in a 30-foot boat he built with his own hands, departing from Peru and spending 45 days alone across the vast Pacific Ocean.

That journey — of courage, craftsmanship, and unwavering faith in something greater — is the same spirit that built Eco Village. Mau and Paola created this ranch not as an escape from the world, but as an invitation to it: come see what is possible when you live in harmony with nature.

"The ocean taught me patience. The land taught me responsibility. Now I share both with anyone willing to listen."

— Mau

Mau and Paola are sailing again — sharing stories from the sea, the land, and life at Eco Village on their blog, Dulcinea Adventure.
